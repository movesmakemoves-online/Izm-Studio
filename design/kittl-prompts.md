## IMPORTANT — how these must be labelled on the site

These are **sample / concept pieces**, not work delivered for real paying clients. They need to
be labelled that way on the Work page (e.g. a small "Sample piece" tag), for two reasons:

1. It's honest — passing off concept work as delivered client projects is misleading
   advertising, and it's the kind of thing that damages trust badly if a client finds out.
2. It's completely normal — designers show concept and personal work all the time. Nobody
   minds a labelled sample. They mind a fake case study.

Real client work (Hand Made By You, Little VIPs) is already on the Work page and correctly
labelled as in-development.

## About-page image — do you need one, and can it be AI?

**Short answer: a real photo of you is the strongest option by a distance, and it's the only
one that carries no risk.**

Here's the honest breakdown:

- **A real photo of you** — best for trust. You're selling direct, personal access ("you deal
  with me, not an account manager"), and a real face backs that up. Doesn't need to be
  professional; a decent phone photo in good natural light, plain background, works fine.
- **An AI-generated portrait presented as you or as "the team"** — don't. Showing an invented
  person as your team on a business site is misleading advertising territory, and it directly
  undercuts the honesty angle the whole About page is now built on. If a client ever met you
  and you didn't match the photo, that's a hard thing to explain.
- **No photo at all** — perfectly acceptable. Plenty of solo consultants run photo-free About
  pages and lean on the writing instead.
- **AI-generated abstract or illustrative imagery** — completely fine, and a good middle ground
  if you don't want your face on the site. It's clearly decorative, not pretending to be a
  person. Prompt below.

### If you want a real photo, keep it simple
Natural light from a window, plain uncluttered background, chest-up, look at the camera, no
heavy filters. Shot on a phone is fine.

### If you'd rather go abstract — Kittl prompt

> A warm, abstract illustration for a creative studio's about page. Warm off-white background
> (#faf8f3) with a large triangular prism shape in deep teal (#0f766e) on the left, splitting
> a beam of light into five soft rays that fan across the frame in teal and amber tones.
> Subtle paper grain, flat modern vector style with soft gradients, calm and inviting rather
> than technical. No people, no faces, no text.

### If you want a workspace shot instead (also a real, honest option)

> A warm, natural-light photograph of a tidy creative workspace: a laptop, a notebook, a
> coffee, and a camera lens on a light wooden desk, shot from above at a slight angle. Soft
> daylight from the left, warm neutral tones, shallow depth of field. Lived-in but not messy.
> No people in frame.

(That last one is fine to generate with AI — an anonymous desk isn't pretending to be a
specific real place or person. But an actual photo of your own desk is even easier.)
