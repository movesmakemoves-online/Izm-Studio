# Competitor Research — Services, Marketing, and Revenue Ideas

Based on the real agencies turned up while researching business names (Forge
Digital Marketing, Forge Worldwide, Cortex Digital/Creative, CRO:NYX Digital,
Flux Digital Labs, Vexel Labs, Pixel Lab UK, Bytery, Digital Creators Studio,
and others) — what they actually sell, and what that means for growing revenue.

## Services these agencies offer that Izm Studio currently doesn't

Current site sells: graphic design, web/app/game dev, video editing, AI
services. Gaps worth considering, all seen repeatedly across real competitors:

1. **SEO** — came up in nearly every agency researched (CRO:NYX, Digital
   Creativity Studios, Flux Labs). Pairs naturally with web builds — a client
   who just paid for a website is the easiest person to sell SEO to next.
2. **Email marketing & automation** — Forge Digital Marketing and Flux Digital
   Labs both lead with this. Genuinely underused by small agencies, ties
   directly to a client's revenue (not just "looks nice"), easy to justify a
   retainer for.
3. **HubSpot / CRM & MarTech setup** — a recurring pattern: Pixel Lab UK is a
   "HubSpot Diamond Partner," CRO:NYX is a "HubSpot Platinum Partner," Flux
   Digital Labs does HubSpot CRM + RevOps. Becoming a certified partner for a
   tool like HubSpot is free/cheap to do and instantly signals credibility —
   worth investigating as a specific credential to pursue.
4. **Paid social / social media management retainers** — content calendars,
   scheduling, community management (Pixel Labs Iowa: "social-first
   marketing").
5. **UI/UX product design as its own line** — not just "web design," but
   ongoing design-system/product work for existing digital products (Forge
   Studio SF specializes only in this).
6. **Branding/brand strategy as an explicit named service** — several
   competitors sell this separately from "design," even though it overlaps —
   worth naming it directly on the site rather than folding it into design.

## Marketing patterns worth copying

- Several of these agencies build their *whole* positioning around one
  credential (e.g. "HubSpot Partner") rather than trying to say everything at
  once. Worth considering one certification/partnership to anchor around.
- Case studies and named client work appear on every single competitor site —
  reinforces that filling in the currently-placeholder Work page is a real
  priority, not just polish.
- Agencies with a specific named niche (Vexel Labs: "design, web applications,
  video editing" — almost identical scope to Izm Studio) read as more
  credible than fully generic "we do everything" positioning, even when the
  actual service list is similar in breadth.

## How to actually make more money — the retainer model

Real 2026 data: **78% of agencies now run primarily on retainers** (up from
64% in 2023), specifically because it's the difference between one-off
project income (unpredictable, feast-or-famine) and predictable monthly cash
flow.

**Why it matters financially:** retainer clients pay for *predictability and
priority access*, not just hours — so retainers should be priced at a
**15–25% premium** over the equivalent one-off project cost, not a discount.
Real benchmark pricing: small clients £1,000–5,000/mo, mid-market
£5,000–15,000/mo. (These are US figures reported in USD — treat as a rough
ceiling/floor, not exact UK pricing.)

**Concrete idea for the Packages page:** add a 4th option alongside
Basic/Standard/Enhanced — a **monthly retainer tier** (e.g. "ongoing design +
dev support, X hours/month, priority turnaround") rather than only one-off
project pricing. This is the single highest-leverage change available given
the research — it turns one-time clients into recurring income instead of
needing to constantly find new one-off projects.
